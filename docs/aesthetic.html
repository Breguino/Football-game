What actually makes it look like FC 26

Seven rules

Strip away the licences and the polygon count and the identity comes down to seven decisions. Miss any one and the whole thing reads as a knock-off.

The background is actually black

Not #111, not charcoal. Menus sit on #000000 — 96% of sampled background pixels were pure black — with a barely-visible blurred stadium interior behind them at 3–6% luminance. Panels float on the void.

Colour is earned

The UI stays black, white and one grey until something is focused, live, or team-owned. Only then: the teal ring, the azure stamina line, the green skill pips, the club's own colours in the scoreboard. Nothing is coloured decoratively.

Light is the interaction

Focus isn't a fill — it's an iridescent light leak escaping above and below the row, cyan through white to warm gold, plus a 1.5px teal outline. It reads as refraction. This is the single most identifying detail in the entire interface.

Labels whisper, values shout

Sentence-case regular label on the left, BOLD UPPERCASE VALUE on the right. That one rhythm carries the whole menu system. Don't tidy it into consistency — the contrast is the design.

Cuts, not corners

Where two coloured blocks meet, they meet on a chevron — a > cut at roughly 63°, descended from the triangle above the controlled player's head. Everything else is generously rounded: 10px rows, 20px panels.

The type leans forward

Display type is a squarish geometric sans at ~9–11° forward oblique, uppercase, tightly tracked, with flat angular terminals. Body type is upright and deliberately plain. The two roles never mix.

The match layer is television

HUD elements are small, cornered, and imitate a real broadcast graphics package. Total HUD ink stays under 8% of the frame. The pitch is the picture; the interface is the lower-third.

Aesthetic fidelity is the primary metric; depth is the secondary one

Build it in three tiers

A gorgeous shallow build beats an ugly deep one. Don't start a tier until the previous one passes its exit criterion.

Tier one

The Shell

  • Boot sequence, hub, squad, settings, match setup
  • Full presentation layer: walkout, kickoff, HUD, replays, full-time
  • Playable 5-a-side on simplified physics

Exit: a stranger sees a screenshot and asks if it's the new FC.

Tier two

The Football

  • 11v11, offside, fouls, set pieces
  • Ball physics with spin and drag; momentum dribbling
  • Team AI: shape, pressing lines, off-ball runs
  • Stamina, cards, subs, in-match tactics

Exit: a match is worth replaying for its own sake.

Tier three

The Modes

  • Career: season sim, table, transfers, training
  • Collection mode: packs, card items, chemistry
  • Cup brackets, local multiplayer

Exit: there's a reason to come back tomorrow.

The one hard constraint

Original identity only

Everything in the FC 26 look that can be lawfully reproduced is design language: the layout grammar, the palette, the geometry, the motion curves, the typographic scale. Everything that can't is identity: club names, crests, kits, player names and likenesses, competition marks, the EA and FC logos, and the F37 typefaces themselves.

So the world gets generated: 80–120 fictional clubs across 4–6 fictional leagues, each with a procedural SVG crest, a kit, a three-letter code and a colour pair; fictional player names drawn from per-nation banks. Because nothing can be licensed, generation quality is content quality — and no placeholder ever survives to production, because there are no placeholders.

Provenance. Colour values and geometry were derived by per-pixel sampling and bounding-box measurement of publicly published FC 26 imagery — EA press assets, Steam store captures, and press-outlet screenshots. No source imagery is redistributed here or in the repository; the aesthetic is encoded numerically so the originals aren't needed to build against it.
